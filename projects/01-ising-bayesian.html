Bayesian Inference
on the 2D Ising Model

Monte Carlo simulation of the 2D Ising model and Bayesian inference of its critical point, checked against Onsager's exact solution. A single-lattice fit fails in an instructive way; finite-size scaling recovers \(T_c\), \(\nu\) and \(\beta\).

Try the live demo ↓ Code on GitHub → Archived release (DOI) →
Background

An exact answer to test against

  • The 2D Ising model is the simplest lattice model with a continuous phase transition, and one of the few solved exactly: \(T_c = 2/\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \approx 2.2692\) (Onsager, 1944), the magnetisation (Yang, 1952), \(\beta = 1/8\), \(\nu = 1\). Every estimate can be checked against the truth.
  • Near \(T_c\), local Metropolis updates (Metropolis et al., 1953) decorrelate ever more slowly: critical slowing down (Hohenberg & Halperin, 1977). Cluster algorithms flip whole correlated regions and avoid it (Swendsen & Wang, 1987; Wolff, 1989).
  • On a finite lattice the transition is rounded. Finite-size scaling (Fisher & Barber, 1972) and the Binder cumulant (Binder, 1981) turn the dependence on lattice size into the estimator.
Approach

Simulate, then infer

  • Simulation: Metropolis (Numba) on a 32×32 lattice at 25 temperatures; Wolff cluster updates for \(L = 16\)–\(128\) at 15 temperatures near \(T_c\). Every run seeded.
  • Errors: blocking and jackknife over blocks of at least 20 autocorrelation times (Metropolis: τ ≈ 1 700 sweeps at \(L = 64\); Wolff: 1.3–1.8).
  • Model 1: the power law \(M \propto (1 - T/T_c)^{\beta}\) on one lattice, heteroscedastic likelihood, flat prior on \(\beta\).
  • Model 2: Binder cumulant and magnetisation of all sizes as functions of \((T - T_c)\,L^{1/\nu}\), flat priors, fit range set by a \(\chi^2\) test.
  • Sampling: random-walk and adaptive Metropolis in NumPy (PyMC NUTS as an alternative); \(\hat R\) and ESS with ArviZ.

One lattice is not enough

Loading magnetization curve…
Simulated ⟨|M|⟩(T) on a 32×32 lattice with the posterior power-law fit; amber line: Onsager's \(T_c\).
  • The power-law fit misses: \(T_c\) comes out 0.1 too high (95 % HDI [2.333, 2.434]) and \(\beta\) moves with the fit window (0.095 → 0.04).
  • The simulation is right, the model is not: below \(T = 2.25\) the data match Onsager's exact magnetisation within 1.8 standard errors. With the first correction of Onsager's expansion the same data give \(\beta = 0.124\).

Finite-size scaling

Fig 2 · Binder crossing and data collapse. The cumulants of all sizes cross at \(T_c\); with the fitted exponents, every size falls on one curve.
  • All three exponents recovered from Wolff data with flat priors; Binder crossing \(U^* = 0.6112(3)(3)\) against the exact 0.6107.
  • Fit range chosen by the data: the widest window that passes a \(\chi^2\) test (\(L \ge 32\), \(|x| \le 1\), \(Q = 0.22\)).
  • Healthy samplers: \(\hat R \le 1.002\), effective sample sizes above 2 600.

Limits

What the results do not show

  • The statistical interval of \(\beta\) alone excludes 1/8; agreement relies on the systematic error, which leaves out corrections whose amplitude varies with the scaling variable (up to +0.004 in \(\beta\)).
  • \(L = 16\) and wider windows fail the \(\chi^2\) test: at this precision the corrections to scaling are resolved, and one correction term does not absorb them.
