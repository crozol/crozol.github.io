02 / 05 Deep learning · Dynamical systems Completed

Neural ODEs for
dynamical systems

Neural networks that learn the vector field of an ODE from short noisy trajectories, and a controlled ablation of when a Hamiltonian prior helps them generalise to orbits they never saw.

Try the live demo ↓ Code on GitHub → Archived release (DOI) →
Background

Learning the law, not the trajectory

  • A Neural ODE learns the right-hand side \(dy/dt = f_\theta(y)\) and integrates it with an adaptive solver, with gradients through the solver or the adjoint equation (Chen et al., 2018; Pontryagin, 1962).
  • Physics priors build structure into \(f_\theta\): a Hamiltonian Neural Network learns a scalar \(H_\theta\) and moves along its symplectic gradient, so it conserves \(H_\theta\) by construction (Greydanus et al., 2019; also Cranmer et al., 2020; Zhong et al., 2020).
  • Open question: when does the prior help? Comparisons often change the architecture and the coordinates at once, and test on the orbit the model was trained on.
Approach

Two systems, one controlled ablation

  • Systems: a damped pendulum and a Lotka–Volterra predator–prey model, observed with Gaussian noise over a short window, then extrapolated.
  • Model: an MLP vector field (3 × 96, tanh, 19 106 parameters) integrated by Dormand–Prince (torchdiffeq), with a curriculum on the integration window.
  • Ablation: the MLP, the same MLP in log-coordinates (the coordinate control) and an HNN of the same size; trained on 1 or 3 trajectories, 5 seeds each, scored on 5 orbits never seen.
  • Loss: the exact likelihood of the observations for every model; the initial states are fitted jointly with the network.

Results

Key results

Ablation on unseen orbits

Fig 2 · Error and drift on five unseen orbits (bars: means; dots: seeds) and the orbits predicted by the log-coordinate MLP and the HNN.
  • The prior pays off with enough data: trained on three trajectories, the HNN halves both error and drift on orbits it never saw; its orbits stay closed, the MLP's spiral.
  • Not with one trajectory: the HNN's orbits still close (drift 12 % against 66–83 %), but they are the wrong orbits.
  • The naive comparison was confounded: on the training orbit, most of the HNN's apparent gain came from measuring the loss in log-coordinates, not from the Hamiltonian structure.
  • The residual drift is a fitting error: tightening the solver tolerance from \(10^{-5}\) to \(10^{-8}\) leaves it unchanged, while the HNN's own \(H_\theta\) is conserved to \(3 \cdot 10^{-5}\).

Limits

What the results do not show

  • The HNN applies only to systems known to be Hamiltonian, in known canonical coordinates; the dissipative pendulum needs a split flow (Sosanya & Greydanus, 2022).
  • Five seeds and five test orbits: the HNN's advantage is clear in ranks (p = 0.008), not a precise effect size.
